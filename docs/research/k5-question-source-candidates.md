# K–5 math questions from authoritative sources

Research date: September 27, 2026. Research and recommendations, not an accepted implementation decision. `CONTEXT.md` currently describes a personal-use game. Commercial reuse is considered below to preserve future options, not because commercialization is an established requirement.

## Recommended starting point

Use **first-edition IM K–5 Math, authored by Illustrative Mathematics and hosted by Kendall Hunt**, as the initial curriculum and question source. It combines a coherent progression with practice and assessment materials. Crucially, IM explicitly distinguishes the first edition's **CC BY 4.0** license, which permits commercial adaptation with attribution, from the newer v.360 edition's **CC BY-NC 4.0** license. Retain edition, source, license, and modification records for every imported question. [IM terms, effective May 21, 2026](https://illustrativemathematics.org/terms-of-use/).

This is a recommendation about content provenance and curricular structure. It is not evidence that completing a particular number of questions establishes mastery.

## Practical distinctions that matter

### IM offers a useful assessment structure, with access limitations

The first-edition guidance provides lesson practice beginning in Kindergarten Unit 2, section checkpoints of three to four problems in grades 2–5, and observational checklists in K–1. Written end-of-unit assessments begin in Kindergarten Unit 2; K–2 assessments may be read aloud. This supports tracking specific learning goals instead of counting arbitrary problems. [How to assess progress](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/teacher-guide/how-to-assess-progress.html).

IM's summative assessments include standard alignment, solutions, varied response types, and different levels of difficulty. These are curriculum assessments, not a claim that an adapted game's score is nationally normed. [Summative assessment design](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/teacher-guide/summative-assessments.html).

The inspected Grade 3 page exposes problem text and standard identifiers, but formatted solutions request a teacher account with a valid work email. The assessment page has the same restriction. Plan a legitimate access route before depending on these materials; this research did not log in or establish bulk download/API access. [Practice example](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-3/unit-3/practice.html), [Assessment example](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-3/unit-3/assessments.html).

### Keep publisher editions separate

Do not substitute the IM task bank, v.360, or Open Up modifications for first-edition questions while carrying over a CC BY label. The current Open Up page explicitly identifies noncommercial modifications and the separate underlying IM license. Its K–5 series is also largely an adaptation of IM, not an independent second validation source. Check image attributions separately. [Open Up licensing](https://www.openupresources.org/help-support/licensing-questions/).

Eureka Math updates the original EngageNY curriculum while keeping its structure and sequence. Great Minds offers printed workbooks, teacher editions, assessment and fluency materials, including K–8 Learn/Practice/Succeed books. These provide an established textbook source, but buying a workbook does not establish permission to republish it inside a game. [EngageNY relationship](https://greatminds.org/math/eurekamath/engageny/new-york), [Print materials](https://greatminds.org/math/eurekamath/print-materials), [Current terms](https://greatminds.org/legal/terms-of-service).

### Rights and acquisition options

For a personal-use prototype, inspect the exact noncommercial license of any chosen alternative and preserve its notices. For possible commercial distribution, first-edition IM is the clearest option verified here. If newer v.360 content is preferred, IM explicitly offers a route to inquire about a limited commercial license; pricing was not established. No commercial license or permission was requested during this research. [IM licensing terms](https://illustrativemathematics.org/terms-of-use/).

MLC's books are useful direct resources for parents, but incorporating them as a game database requires a separate permission route under the inspected terms. [K–5 practice books](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/educators/free-resources/lessons-publications/practice-books), [Website terms](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/terms).

## Suggested content pilot

Start with one grade and a few related skills before importing the whole curriculum. For each item record the source URL, edition, unit/section/problem identifier, standard, intended skill, answer/rubric, diagrams and their licenses, response format, and whether the item is verbatim or adapted. Keep original-source questions distinguishable from internally authored variants.

Have an elementary math educator review question selection and any adaptations. Moving a written explanation or manipulative task into a multiple-choice game can change what it measures. A numerical-answer subset can still be useful, but should not stand in for every mathematical practice in the source curriculum.

The current between-wave quiz uses five questions under a shared 30-second countdown. Many textbook reasoning tasks will need an untimed context or another interaction format. Choosing authoritative sources alone will not resolve that mismatch.

## Parent reporting: completion, coverage and demonstrated learning

Recommendation, not a validated assessment model: report three separate quantities.

1. **Practice completed:** total attempts, distinct questions and sessions. Repeated questions and correction rounds count as practice, not new independent evidence.
2. **Coverage:** named skills/standards practiced and assessed, with an explicit denominator for the selected curriculum/unit. A percentage of the app's selected skills is not automatically a percentage of an entire grade.
3. **Demonstrated learning:** first-attempt performance on varied, previously unseen items, across sessions, followed by a later retention check. Show insufficient evidence and needs-review states.

Question volume alone cannot establish mastery: repeated easy questions, hints, retries and memorized answers can inflate completion without demonstrating transfer. An authoritative textbook supplies defensible content and sequence; it does not validate a new game's scoring model.

A proposed initial workflow is: brief baseline → targeted practice → unseen skill checkpoint → delayed review → mixed unit checkpoint. Use number sentences, visual models and word problems where appropriate, preserving what the source item is meant to assess. K–2 tasks may need manipulatives, audio or adult observation; record assistance and distinguish reading demands from mathematical demands.

As a **provisional pilot rule only**, a narrowly defined skill might be marked “demonstrated” after at least 10 of 12 independent first attempts are correct across three sessions and multiple representations, then “retained” after at least 4 of 5 new questions are correct 7–14 days later. These counts, percentages and intervals are product hypotheses, not publisher-endorsed or scientifically established thresholds. A mathematics educator should review each skill's coverage and scoring rubric; pilot results should inform thresholds. Broader standards require more than one narrow skill checkpoint. Recheck older evidence rather than treating retained status as permanent.

Spacing and retrieval practice have research support, but that support does not validate the example thresholds above. The IES practice guide recommends spacing learning and using quizzes to re-expose learners to key content. [IES/What Works Clearinghouse guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1).

Illustrative parent report, with invented numbers:

> 120 questions completed across 8 skills. Six skills demonstrated; four also retained on later checks. Addition within 20: 11/12 correct independently across three sessions, then 5/5 on new questions one week later. Next focus: subtraction word problems. Source: named curriculum, edition, unit and lesson.

For baseline-to-current comparisons, use different questions matched for skill coverage and difficulty, show the actual counts and dates, and avoid presenting a raw accuracy increase across unequal quizzes as a standardized growth score.

Do not label results “NAEP proficient,” national percentile, grade equivalent or certified mastery based on borrowed released questions. NCES explicitly states NAEP does not report individual student scores. [NCES interpretation guidance](https://nces.ed.gov/nationsreportcard/guides/). Such claims require an appropriate validated assessment and administration/scoring process.

## Fit with Math on Mars

Current project context describes five-question between-wave quizzes sharing a 30-second countdown, with speed and first-attempt accuracy affecting reward power level. My recommendation is to keep educational evidence distinct from reward quality: speed may be relevant to a deliberately assessed fluency skill, but it is not sufficient evidence of conceptual understanding.

Longer source questions and explanations need an untimed checkpoint experience or a deliberate revision of the current quiz design. This is a proposal to revisit the existing behavior in CONTEXT.md, not an implemented change. Correction rounds remain valuable practice but must not overwrite first-attempt assessment evidence.

For every curated item retain source URL, edition, unit/lesson/item identifier, attribution/license, original-versus-adapted status, mapped standard and narrow skill, answer/rubric, representation type and editorial review. If wording or numbers change, label it “adapted from,” preserve provenance, and review the adaptation; publisher authorship of the original is not endorsement of the game's version.

Start with one grade and one unit using first-edition IM, reserving distinct items for checkpoints and delayed review. Audit coverage and have an elementary math educator review the selection before expanding to K–5. If a turnkey bank is preferable, investigate Instructure's Channel Program in parallel. No purchases, provider outreach, content imports or app changes were made for this research.
