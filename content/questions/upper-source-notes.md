# Upper-grade starter bank provenance

Reviewed 2026-09-27. `im-upper.json` contains **81 fixed, adapted practice items**: grade 3 (25), grade 4 (21), grade 5 (35). Each preserves the numbers in the cited public problem. These are selected numerical subparts or adaptations, not reproductions of the complete reasoning tasks or a full grade curriculum.

Sources read:

- [IM Grade 3, Unit 4 practice](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-3/unit-4/practice.html): multiplication, missing factors, equal sharing, grouping, larger-number multiplication and division. 25 items; publisher standard 3.OA.A.4 verified on Section A Problem 4 and 3.OA.A.3 on Section A Problem 5.
- [IM Grade 4, Unit 3 practice](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-4/unit-3/practice.html): whole-number multiplication of fractions, subtraction, mixed numbers, tenths and hundredths. 21 items. Some explanation/comparison questions are converted to calculation; recorded in each item's `source.changes`.
- [IM Grade 5, Unit 5 practice](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-5/unit-5/practice.html): decimal addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and rounding. 25 items.
- [IM Grade 5, Unit 3 practice](https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-5/unit-3/practice.html): multiplication of fractions and division involving unit fractions. 10 items.

The name and logo are excluded from that license. No logos, third-party images, or gated teacher solutions are copied. Hints and explanations are app-authored calculations, not publisher solutions. Original source excerpts use plain-text math notation in place of the page's typeset notation.

Per-item standards are left empty where the public practice page does not explicitly show a publisher mapping. Skill names are app-authored descriptive labels. Grade means source curriculum grade; this sample does not establish full-grade coverage or validated mastery. Some problems are too involved for eight seconds, especially text problems and multi-step arithmetic. Prefer an adjustable pace and distinguish practiced item counts from independently demonstrated skill retention.
